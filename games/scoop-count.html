Scoop Count

The recipe needs an awkward amount and only some of your measuring cups are clean. Use the cups you have to measure exactly the right amount — in as few scoops as you can.

How to play

  1. The amount the recipe needs is shown at the top, for example 2 5/12 cups.
  2. Tap a cup to add one full scoop of it to the bowl. You can use the same cup as many times as you like.
  3. Reach the exact amount to finish. Going over does not count — tap Undo to take the last scoop back.
  4. Any exact answer is a win, but try to match the fewest scoops number. On a keyboard, press 1–4 for the cups and Backspace to undo.

Tips

  • Think in twelfths. Every cup here is a whole number of twelfths: 1 cup = 12, 3/4 = 9, 2/3 = 8, 1/2 = 6, 1/3 = 4 and 1/4 = 3. So 2 5/12 cups is 29 twelfths, and the puzzle becomes adding small whole numbers.
  • Biggest first is not always best. To make 1 1/3 cups (16 twelfths) with 3/4, 2/3 and 1/4 cups, starting with 3/4 leaves 7 twelfths, and no mix of 1/4 cups can finish that. Two 2/3 scoops do it straight away.
  • Fix the leftover. Look at the part after the whole cups. Thirds (4/12, 8/12) can only come from 1/3 and 2/3 cups, or from mixing 1/4 and 3/4 cups with others. Work out which cups can make that leftover, then fill the rest with the biggest cup.

Frequently asked questions

Is there always an answer?

Yes. A program works out the fewest scoops for every board before it is shown, and only uses boards that can be measured exactly in 3 to 7 scoops. Many days are chosen so that simply grabbing the biggest cup first does not give the shortest answer.

Why these cup sizes?

They are the sizes found in most US measuring-cup sets: 1, 3/4, 2/3, 1/2, 1/3 and 1/4 cup. Each day three or four of them are available.

When does a new puzzle appear?

The daily recipe is based on the date on your own device, so it changes at midnight your time. Everyone on the same date gets the same recipe.

Do you save my results or track me?

No. The puzzle runs entirely in your browser. It does not use cookies, does not store anything on your device, and sends nothing to us.

Is this useful for real cooking?

It is good practice for adding fractions, which comes up when you scale a recipe up or down. For baking where accuracy really matters, a kitchen scale is still the most reliable tool.
