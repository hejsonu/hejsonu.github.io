Fence Calculator: Posts, Pickets, Rails and Concrete

"Length divided by post spacing" gives you one post too few on a straight run — and on a fence with corners and gates the error grows. This tool counts posts side by side, shares the corner posts, adds a post for each gate, then works out pickets, rails and concrete from the same layout.

How to use it

  1. Measure each straight side of the fence and type the lengths in order, for example 50, 30, 50 for a back yard that starts and ends at the house. Tick the box if the fence goes all the way around and the last side meets the first.
  2. Set the maximum distance between posts. 8 feet is common because rails are sold in 8-foot lengths; windy sites and heavy panels often use 6 feet. The tool spaces posts evenly on each side, so the real spacing is usually a little less than your maximum.
  3. Add up to two gates and the side each one is on. The tool assumes each gate sits at one end of its side, next to a corner or end post. A gate in the middle of a side can need one more post and section.
  4. Enter the picket width and the gap. A privacy fence has no gap; a classic picket fence often has a gap of 2 to 3 inches. Pickets and rails for the gates themselves are not included — gates are usually bought or built separately.
  5. For concrete, enter the hole size. A common rule of thumb is a hole about three times the post width and about a third of the post's length in the ground, but follow your local code and frost depth.

Worked examples

1. A straight 100-foot privacy fence

100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so the run needs 13 sections — and 13 sections need 14 posts, not 13. Spread evenly, the posts end up 7.69 feet apart. With 5½-inch pickets and no gap: 1,200 inches ÷ 5.5 = 218.2, so 219 pickets, or 230 with 5% spare. Two rails per section: 26 rails. With 10-inch holes 30 inches deep around 4x4 posts, each hole takes 1.151 cubic feet of concrete: 16.11 cubic feet in total, which is 43 bags of 50 lb or 27 bags of 80 lb.

2. A back yard: three sides with a gate

Sides of 50, 30 and 50 feet start and end at the house, with a 4-foot gate at the end of the 30-foot side. Picket fence: 3½-inch pickets with 2½-inch gaps.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the post count always one more than the number of sections?

Every section needs a post at both ends, and neighbouring sections share one. So a straight run of n sections has n + 1 posts. On a fence that closes on itself, the last section ends at the first post, so posts and sections are equal. Each gate adds one post, because the opening needs a post on both sides.

How much concrete does one bag make?

The tool uses yields commonly printed on bags of concrete mix: about 0.375 cubic feet for a 50 lb bag and 0.6 cubic feet for an 80 lb bag. Check the label on the product you buy, and buy one or two extra bags — holes are rarely perfectly round.

Do I need gravel in the bottom of the post hole?

Many builders put a few inches of gravel under the post for drainage. If you do, enter its depth and the tool counts concrete only for the part of the hole above it. Follow the instructions on the concrete bag and your local building rules.
