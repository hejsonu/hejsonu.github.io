Paint Calculator

Knowing you need "1.9 gallons" is only half the answer — paint is sold in gallons and quarts, and a quart usually costs far more than a quarter of a gallon. This tool works out the paint for your walls and then the cheapest set of cans that covers it.

How to use it

  1. Measure the floor length and width and the wall height in feet.
  2. Count the doors and windows. The tool subtracts 21 sq ft per door (3 x 7 ft) and 15 sq ft per window (3 x 5 ft); for very large windows or patio doors, count them as two.
  3. Take the coverage from the can — most interior paints say 250–400 sq ft per gallon. Use the lower end for rough or porous walls and for strong color changes.
  4. Two coats is the norm for a new color. Add the prices you see at the store to get the cheapest combination of cans.

Coverage on the label assumes a smooth, primed surface. Fresh drywall, bare plaster and dark-to-light changes can need a primer coat or a third coat. The plan below does not include paint for trim or a primer.

Worked examples

Can prices below are illustrative, not quotes from any store.

1. A 12 x 12 ft bedroom, 8 ft walls, 1 door, 2 windows, 2 coats

Walls: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 sq ft, minus 21 for the door and 30 for the windows = 333 sq ft. Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon: 666 ÷ 350 = 1.90 gallons. With gallons at $45 and quarts at $20, two gallons ($90) are cheaper than one gallon plus four quarts ($125).

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior wall paints list 250–400 sq ft per gallon for one coat. The number on your can is the one to use; textured or unpainted surfaces soak up more.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes for normal rooms — it can save a quart or more. But if the result is right on the edge of a can size, round up rather than down: you will want some left for touch-ups.

Is it cheaper to buy quarts or a gallon?

Per ounce, gallons are almost always cheaper; a quart is a quarter of the paint but often 40–50% of the gallon price. Quarts only make sense to top up a small shortfall, as in example 2. Enter your store's prices and the tool compares every mix.
